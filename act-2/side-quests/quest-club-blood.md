# SIDE QUEST — CLUB BLOOD
*Act 2 side quest. Entry: membership chips on the spawn in Scene 3.*

---

## GM Background

> *For the GM only.*

The party is not the only one who found the chips interesting.

**Tobias Rieper** has been in Night City for three weeks. He arrived with a contract and a cover — a consultant, visiting on corporate business, staying at a City Center hotel that doesn't ask for the kind of identification that goes into databases. His real name is not Tobias Rieper. He has a barcode on the back of his head that he keeps covered. He does not discuss either of these things.

His contract: eliminate a specific individual who attends Club Blood's lower level — one of the ancient members, a Wildemount-era vampire operating under a current cover identity as a minor corporate art consultant. The client is a Night City resident who lost a family member to the Director's removal service eight months ago; she is not wealthy enough to fight Arasaka directly and does not know that is what she is fighting. She knows only that someone arranged her daughter's disappearance, and that the person she is paying an extraordinary amount of money knows how to make problems stop.

The contract should have been straightforward.

It has not been straightforward.

**What went wrong:** 47 has attempted the contract twice. Once with a suppressed weapon, point-blank. Once with a compound that has never failed him. Both times the target continued functioning. The second time, the target looked at him with an expression that suggested mild inconvenience and left the room. 47 has been reassessing for five days. He does not panic. He files anomalies and adjusts his approach. But he needs information he does not currently have, and he knows he needs it.

When the party surfaces at Club Blood following the spawn chips, he watches them for approximately twenty minutes before deciding they are more useful to him than dangerous.

---

## Entry Points

**Standard:** The party arrives at Club Blood's surface entrance with the membership chips from the Watson tunnels. The chips are old enough to attract attention. Someone at the door will want to know how they came by them. Getting into the lower level requires either a convincing story or a connection — a scene in itself.

**If the party's notoriety is already high:** the surface club is accessible through the same notoriety network that opened the Afterlife. Club Blood's ownership is separate, but the name carries. They get the surface floor without the chip conversation.

**47's entry point:** He is already inside. Surface floor, corner table, a glass of water he has not touched. He is dressed in a manner that makes him invisible in this room — expensive, precise, the kind of person who belongs in an expensive club without being notable in one. He is watching the door to the access corridor.

---

## Beat 1 — First Contact

47 approaches when the party has been inside long enough that he's made his assessment. He will not approach them in a crowd. He will wait for a quieter moment — a corridor, a table away from the main floor, the access route to the lower level if they're headed that way.

He sits, or stands close enough to speak quietly, and says:

> *"You're here because of the chips. Watson tunnels, eastern corridor. Three of them."*

Not a question. He already knows.

> *"I've been watching this location for three weeks. You're the first people who have arrived with those chips and appear to still be alive. I want to understand how that happened."*

He is not threatening. He is gathering information. There is a difference in posture that is very clear.

**What he offers immediately:** he has a contract in this building. The target is on the lower level. He has attempted to complete the contract twice and encountered results that do not correspond to his experience or his research. He needs an explanation for what he is dealing with. In exchange, he will share everything he knows about Club Blood — membership, layout, patterns, the shape of the arrangement with Arasaka — which is considerable.

**What he does not offer:** his client's identity. The terms of the contract. His real name. None of these are relevant to the information exchange he is proposing.

**His tone throughout:** entirely professional. No warmth, no hostility, no apparent stake beyond the functional. He describes his two failed attempts with the same precision he would use to describe weather. *"Point-blank suppressed. The target continued. I filed it and withdrew."* He does not understand why. That gap is what he is here to close.

---

## Beat 2 — The Exchange

The party explains what they are dealing with. 47 listens without interrupting.

When they finish:

> *"Stake through the heart. Decapitation. Fire. Sunlight."*

A pause.

> *"These are verified methods, not folklore."*

If the party confirms: he accepts this without visible reaction. He files it. He has operated against enhanced targets, cyberware-heavy targets, targets with military-grade protection. This is new data, not a different category of problem.

> *"That explains the anomalies. The compound I used should have disrupted cardiac function. It did not, because the mechanism is different. The suppressed round — the target's tissue response was wrong."*

He is not shaken. He is recalibrating.

**What he shares in exchange:**

- Club Blood has been operating under various names for at least forty years. The shell ownership structure goes back further than that — pre-corporate incorporation, which means someone was planning long-term.
- The lower level has a controlled membership of between twelve and eighteen individuals. He has visually confirmed nine. The ancient members — the ones who have been here for longer than the club — he has identified three. His target is one.
- The arrangement with Arasaka: he traced it through contract routing. Removal orders that originate in Arasaka's mid-tier executive structure and route through three shells before landing with an independent operator. The pattern is distinctive. He has seen it eleven times in his research. He cannot prove what it is, but he knows its shape.
- Physical access to the lower level: the door in the utility corridor, the layout below (he's been down once, briefly, before withdrawing when the target made him), the position of the members when they gather.
- On the Arasaka Tower: he has had contracts inside Arasaka before. He knows the security architecture of the Tower's lower and mid-levels. He will share this *"if the current matter concludes to mutual satisfaction."*

> *GM note: the Tower schematics are the real prize here. 47 is dangling them. He will deliver if the party helps him complete this contract — or at minimum, if they help him understand what he needs to understand to complete it himself.*

---

## Beat 3 — The Decision

The party knows what they know from `locations/club-blood.md`. The ancient members are potential information sources: they knew the Director before the transformation. One of them may know the Archivist's name. Killing 47's target may close that thread permanently.

**Option A — Help him complete the contract**

The party assists. They know how to kill it; he has the access and the patience. Together, they plan the elimination. The ancient vampire member dies.

*What the party loses:* one potential information source. Whatever that member knew about the Director's original name, the Archivist's identity, Wildemount — gone.

*What the party gains:* 47's full cooperation. He delivers the Tower schematics. He offers one additional professional service for the raid — a specific obstacle, a specific target, cleared before the party goes in. He does not offer more than this. He is not joining anyone's cause. He is settling a debt.

*Complication:* the Director's arrangement with Club Blood is now disrupted. The remaining ancient members know something happened to one of their own. Whether they connect it to the party depends on how cleanly the operation goes.

**Option B — Negotiate a delay**

The party makes a case: the target has information they need first. A name, or two. They ask 47 to hold the contract while they extract it.

47 considers this. He will agree if the case is good — he is professional, not inflexible, and a delay that produces better intelligence is sometimes the right call. His conditions: a specific timeline (he names one; the party can negotiate), and confirmation that his contract completes when the window closes. He will not cancel. He will wait.

*What this enables:* the party approaches the ancient member and attempts to extract the information they need — the Director's original name, potentially the Archivist's name — before 47 completes his contract. This is a separate beat, and a dangerous one (see below).

*What this risks:* 47 is not flexible indefinitely. If the party takes too long, he proceeds without them, and they lose the window.

**Option C — Approach the target directly**

The party bypasses 47's contract and goes to the ancient member themselves — tells them there's a hitter in the building, offers to help them survive it, in exchange for what they know. The vampire will find this interesting. They have been alive a very long time. They do not panic easily.

*What this enables:* a direct conversation with the oldest thing in Night City, who knew the Director as a mortal practitioner and may have known the Archivist as a colleague. The information could be significant.

*What this risks:* 47 becomes a complication rather than an asset. He notices what the party is doing. He is professional enough not to burn the operation, but the debt dissolves. The Tower schematics go with it. And the ancient vampire may not be trustworthy — they have survived this long by being good at it.

---

## The Ancient Member — A Name for the Table

The contract target: **Mirela**. Cover identity: Vera Ashford, independent corporate art consultant with a small Heywood office and a client list of Night City's wealthy. She has been in Night City for longer than the name. She has been in the world for longer than Night City.

She looks roughly forty. She moves like someone who has never once been startled in their life.

She knows the Director's original name. She has known it since before it was a secret. She also knows the Archivist — not as an AI fragment, but as the wizard he was, the one who tried to get out before the Director closed the board. She does not know he survived.

> *"He survived? In what form?"*

If the party tells her: the weight of this lands differently on her than anything else they could have said. She is quiet for a long time. Whatever she says next, it is the most honest thing she has said in decades.

**What she will trade for, if the party is negotiating:**
- Protection from the current contract (she does not fear 47 on principle, but she is pragmatic about odds)
- Confirmation that the party will not assist in eliminating her
- In return: the Director's original name. What he was before. What he wanted from the soul-drain before it became what it is now.

She will not trade the Archivist's name easily. She will want to know why they want it. What they are to him. If the party's answer is the right one — *we know him, we are trying to restore him* — she considers.

> *"His name was — [she says it]. He would not want me to give it to someone who did not understand what it cost him to lose it."*

> *"If you find him. If he remembers. Tell him Mirela sends her regards. He will know what that means."*

---

## 47 at the End

However the operation concludes, 47 does not linger.

If the contract completes: he thanks them in the manner of someone who does not often have occasion to do so. He provides the Tower schematics. He states the terms of the one additional service offer clearly, without elaboration. He leaves.

If the contract is deferred and eventually completed: same as above, slightly later.

If the contract falls apart entirely — if the party actively protects his target: he is not angry. He does not express emotion about it. He notes that the debt does not apply, the schematics stay with him, and he withdraws from Night City on his own timeline. He may return, on his own terms, if the party's activities intersect with a future contract. He files them as complex variables in a city full of them.

> *"You made a choice. So did I, when I took the contract. Neither of us was wrong, exactly."*

He leaves. He does not explain further.
