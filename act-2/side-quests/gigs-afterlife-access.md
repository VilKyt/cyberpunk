# GIGS — AFTERLIFE ACCESS
*Three street-cred jobs. 2 of 3 gets the party through the Afterlife door.*

---

## Overview

The Afterlife doesn't advertise entry requirements. It doesn't need to. The door staff know who matters in Night City and who doesn't. The party arrives from the Badlands fringe with no track record — they don't exist yet.

That changes with work. Three jobs are available in Watson and the surrounding districts that, done visibly and done well, will put the party's name in circulation among the fixers and mercs who drink at the Afterlife. Complete any two of the three and Rogue's door staff will have heard of them. The third, if they do it, is extra — it earns a warmer reception rather than just entry.

These are not Rogue's jobs. They are standard fixer work available through Night City's underground circuit. The reputation they generate is organic. Word of mouth. The right people talking to the right people.

---

## GIG A — THE RACE

**Source:** Takemura's cousin's rival's fixer — it doesn't matter. Someone who runs Night City's underground circuit puts the word out.

**The job:** Enter a night race through Watson's industrial corridor. Three laps, multiple vehicles, no rules enforced past the start line. The circuit runs through active streets — traffic is a factor, not a problem to be avoided.

**What makes it a street-cred gig:** The field includes established Night City names — people who are known, who will talk afterward. Placing first, or winning in a way that gets remembered (dramatic overtake, handling a deliberate ram attempt, that moment in lap two where someone's car loses a door), puts the party in conversations at the Afterlife bar within forty-eight hours.

**Complications:**
- One of the other drivers has been paid to force a specific car off the road — the party might not know this until it happens to them or someone else
- The route passes within two blocks of an Arasaka checkpoint. Brief, survivable, but the party will need to make a call about whether to show their faces or route around it
- Post-race, the losing fixer tries to recover equipment from the winning vehicle. Standard. Easily handled. But it establishes whether the party's word is good under mild pressure.

**The street-cred beat:** A fixer named Emmett — old, fat, permanently stationed at the Afterlife bar — bets on races and loses badly. He was at the finish line. He will tell anyone who sits near him exactly what happened, in detail, with embellishment. His embellishments always make the winner sound better than the reality. This is why his bets keep losing.

## GIG B — THE WAREHOUSE

**Source:** A mid-tier fixer, working through encrypted channels, wants something retrieved from a corporate-leased warehouse on Watson's industrial waterfront.

**The job:** A shipping container in bay 7 holds six crates of military-grade medical supplies — not Arasaka, not Militech, a smaller corp that leases the space. The supplies were diverted from a humanitarian shipment and are sitting in the container while a shell company figures out how to move them without triggering an audit. The fixer's client wants them before that happens.

**Complications:**
- The warehouse has a security rotation. Three guards, one drone, motion sensors on the bay doors. Standard corporate contractor setup — competent, not elite.
- One of the guards is having a very bad night for personal reasons and is distracted in a way that makes them simultaneously easier to avoid and harder to predict. Their pattern breaks in the third hour.
- The crates are heavier than listed. Something else is in there. The fixer didn't mention it because they didn't know, or because they knew and calculated the party would find out after. Either way, the party makes the call about what to do with what they find.

**What's actually in there (GM decision — choose one):**
- A set of prototype cyberware components Militech thought had been destroyed
- Medical records from a Trauma Team operation that implicate a corp executive in something survivable but embarrassing
- A crate of old weapons with pre-corporate serial numbers that the Archivist, if asked later, will recognise as Wildemount manufacture

**The street-cred beat:** The fixer who hired them pays promptly and adds a notation to their client list. That notation gets referenced when a different fixer asks about reliable Watson operators. Three degrees of separation and it reaches someone who drinks at the Afterlife.

## GIG C — THE KILLER

**Source:** Unofficial. A community board posts it as a missing persons cluster. A fixer with a conscience (they exist) picks it up and offers it as a job.

**The job:** People have been disappearing from a few blocks of Pacifica over the past weeks. Trauma Team found one of them — barely alive, hidden in a sub-basement. The fixer wants the responsible party brought in alive if possible, handled if not.

**What's actually happening:**

The perpetrator is a former Arasaka contractor fitted with an **experimental Arasaka chip** — the Director's attempt to mass-produce soldiers. The chip drains the soul completely and routes the necrotic energy into the body: inhuman strength, very hard to kill. The goal is that the drained subject then obeys. In his case that part failed. He is not cyberpsycho, not malicious. There is simply nobody left inside.

The chip followed the strongest neural associations it could find. The victims are the people he knew best: the barista he saw every morning, his lover, an older neighbour, the people on his floor. His apartment still has the life in it — a mug with the barista's name, a borrowed book never returned.

He missed his check-in. Arasaka is coming to collect him.

**Running the fight:**
- *Body:* Gladiator (MM p.346) base. Immune to charm and fear. Lower AC than you'd expect — he doesn't dodge. High HP.
- *Strength Drain* (as Wight, MM p.300) on hit or grapple: STR save or lose 1d4 STR until a rest.
- *Aura of Soul Erosion (10 ft):* concentrating creatures make a DC 13 CON save at the start of their turn or lose concentration. Magic-sensitive characters see it.
- *The flicker:* every 3 rounds the chip destabilises for one breath — his hands drop, his head tilts, a person for a second. During that round a called shot at the chip is possible (disadvantage; a hit deals double damage to the chip). Destroying it ends him.

**The collection team:** mid-fight, a **Hound** team arrives (see `world/notoriety.md`, Tier 3) — Arasaka's magic-aware unit. They want the subject alive and the chip back, and they do not want the party to have either. Choices: let them take him (the Director learns from the failure), fight both, negotiate a walk-away, or destroy the chip first. If the party has an Arasaka file, the Hounds have a second order about them.

**The chip, if recovered:** first physical proof of what the Director is building. The Archivist can read it. Militech would pay a great deal for it. Club Blood's old members would be alarmed by it.

**The capture vs. kill decision:** The fixer wants alive — there's someone at a university who studies aberrant behaviour and would pay well for a live subject. The community wants him dead. Arasaka wants him back. The party's choice will be remembered.

**The street-cred beat:** This one travels faster than the others. A killer in Pacifica is news. The party being the ones who dealt with it is more news. By the time it reaches the Afterlife, the story has evolved considerably. Emmett will be telling a version of it that is twenty percent accurate and entirely compelling.

---

## The Door

Two of the three done — through any combination — and the door staff at the Afterlife will step aside when the party approaches. No words needed. One of them might say: *"Heard about the warehouse job. Come in."*

Three done, and the bartender has a drink poured before they reach the bar.

Either way, they get to Rogue. What happens next is in [Node 2 — The Afterlife](quest-node-2-the-afterlife.md).
